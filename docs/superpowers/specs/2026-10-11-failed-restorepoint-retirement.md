# Failed-attempt restorepoint retirement — accepted specification

## Root decision — 11 October 2026

Root has now read the complete design, retention audit and feasibility review. Under the user's explicit self-review/implement instruction, this narrow first-version design is accepted for implementation planning: exact-pair best-effort reclamation for authenticated never-committed precommit existing-server failures; physical pins stay until exact deletion; full published-history/final-policy/reference proof and an exact native handle-bound file deletion primitive are required. No retention-only Repair admission block, no intentional delay after proven eligibility, and conservative unreceipted/legacy/independent-owner deferral remain explicit limits. Native primitive and service tests may use generated isolated roots only. This decision does not certify or authorize deletion of either observed historical pair, real instances, or a1 evidence. Product implementation begins only after the executable plan is self-reviewed and the file/test ownership boundaries are assigned; source gates and later normal-app acceptance are still required. This durable specification supersedes the scratch proposal's preparation-only status. Its safety exclusions remain binding.

## Status, intent, and scope

**Accepted for implementation planning on 11 October 2026 under the Root decision above.** The [retention audit](../../../.superpowers/sdd/2026-10-08-matching-client-packs/task-6-restorepoint-retention-audit.md) is the problem statement; the [focused feasibility review](../../../.superpowers/sdd/2026-10-08-matching-client-packs/task-6-retirement-feasibility-review.md) supplies deletion/policy evidence; the [accepted scratch design](../../../.superpowers/sdd/2026-10-08-matching-client-packs/task-6-failed-restorepoint-retirement-design.md) preserves the decision history. Historical line references below describe that review snapshot, not a current implementation. Neither observed archive pair is certified reclaimable or authorized for deletion. Product implementation remains gated on Root's self-review of the executable plan and assignment of file/test ownership.

The requested outcome is automatic best-effort reclamation of eligible failed/cancelled attempts' own archive/sidecar pairs, independent of ordinary backup retention. Completed-operation rollback history, unresolved recovery, and independent owners remain protected. The first supported class is an **existing-server precommit attempt whose commit never started and whose candidate never became canonical**, with authenticated `failed` registry history, `pre-swap-restored`, and `swapStep: none`. Cancellation is a presentation status; the recovery registry records the settled attempt as failed. Exclude `prepared`/commit-started failures, `between-renames-restored`, `rolled-back`, successful rollback operations, completed operations, new-server operations, divergent-policy variants (including uninstall with `possibleNonRunnable`), and uncertainty. Broader disposition/operation support needs separate proof and review.

Recommendation: **no intentional retention delay once eligibility is proven**, using a narrow exact-point operation and durable retirement receipt. Preserve the existing physical operation pin until its sidecar is deleted; archival classification occurs in the receipt, not by unpinning the surviving pair. Attempt exact-handle deletion when the target is safely available, record partial results, and retry authorized work. A receipt or pin classification alone is not reclaimed bytes.

Root's design direction rejects a retention-only admission block: another otherwise-safe and usable server Repair must not be refused merely because reclamation failed. Show a separate retention warning and retain durable retry evidence. Persistent I/O errors, independent owners, and unreceipted crash gaps can leave archives indefinitely; there is no hard count/byte bound or guaranteed cleanup latency. Normal recovery, writer-safety, and capacity checks remain in force. Implementation dispatch remains gated on plan self-review and ownership assignment.

## Ownership policy

An authenticated failed **recovery-registry history** reference becomes archival, non-owning evidence only after all of the following are proved:

1. Exact job, operation kind, existing target identity/path, point ID/digest, and point's creator operation match. This is the attempt's newly created safety snapshot, never a selected older rollback point.
2. Fully published terminal record and target envelope authenticate the permitted never-published disposition. No `restoreCreation`, failed sibling, publication/registration, live candidate mutation, durable commit, unproven worker, or unresolved recovery/cleanup remains. Original retention and owned-candidate removal were authenticated by the source-settlement path.
3. Required source policy restoration and the caller's final policy boundary completed, and runtime writer authority is settled. Initial authorization comes from the live successful `abandonCandidate` plus required outer `restorePriorState` boundary, or an explicitly proved final boundary of actual source recovery. An uncertain finish or failed policy return cannot be papered over with a UI status. Restart discovery without an authorized receipt is deferred; it cannot reconstruct this proof from history alone.
4. The current canonical target/parent and backup directory still bind to the authorized objects. On retry, validate current directory lineage and exact point; an old full-tree fingerprint is historical evidence, not a requirement that a legitimately running original never change again.
5. All server-local lifecycle history pages have no matching point reference; no other active recovery reference, reviewed/claimed plan, or other owner requires it. Unknown or malformed reference evidence fails closed. In the normal first-version production path a fresh failed point is never offered for planning because it has no local history, but this invariant needs a production regression and must not replace checks against tampered/legacy references.

The registry record and operation event history remain readable and immutable. Do not erase their `restorePoint` field to make a reference checker pass. Their archival classification is accompanied by authenticated retirement evidence; presentation distinguishes pending reclamation from confirmed reclamation and never offers an unavailable restore action. Failed/cancelled outcome actions presently do not offer `review-restore-point` (`lifecycle-outcome-actions.cjs:94-107`); preserve that distinction.

For these checks, point identity is target-scoped point ID plus its integrity digest, not digest alone. Creation generates independent IDs/names and hashes only archive bytes, so distinct valid single-linked pairs may share SHA-256. A well-formed different-ID reference is not an owner merely because the bytes match; same-ID digest/locator conflicts, partial/malformed references, uncertain target binding, and known same-physical-file/path aliases remain conservative deferrals. Exact immutable sidecar/lineage/native single-link checks and independent physical pins still protect the candidate. No digest-deduplication scan or relaxation of same-file ownership is authorized.

For the smallest conservative first policy, **another independent owner/reference prevents authorization and deletion**, rather than adding a general ownership-transfer protocol now. Independent pins must never be removed. Preserve the failed operation's physical pin throughout reclamation as a guard against the ordinary cleaner. A protected leftover yields a warning, not a Repair admission block; this may retain multiple pairs. Recheck independent references before each destructive effect, including on retry.

## Compare the approaches

1. **Recommended: exact best-effort reclamation with physical pins retained.** Reuse authenticated registry history, the coordinator, backup lock, reference validators, and durable JSON receipts. Add one narrow retirement helper and native exact-file deletion capability. Production calls it after proven settlement and on defined retries. No sidecar pin CAS, age scanner, configurable retention engine, new daemon, or recursive purge.
2. **Unpin and trigger ordinary retention.** Smaller-looking but not sufficient: ordinary retention may be disabled, preserves archives according to unrelated backup rules, lacks the exact owner/reference proof, and uses a weaker deletion API. It neither supplies this policy nor guarantees a bound.
3. **Dedicated count/byte/age failed-point subsystem.** Could keep diagnostic archives for a time, but needs ordering, quotas, time-based retries, owner/reference indexing, and the same exact deletion protocol. Defer unless the user explicitly wants retained failed snapshots. A count limit alone does not bound bytes for arbitrarily large worlds.

## Minimal durable evidence and exact operation

Use a small `failed-restorepoint-retirement.cjs` helper, not a generic garbage collector. Let `RecoveryRegistry` own its durable metadata alongside its existing root, in a separately validated `retirements` directory; do **not** place new filenames in `active` or `history`, whose current strict filename grammar would classify them as corruption. Reuse `durableFs` atomic JSON/CAS and pinned-directory validation.

### Discoverable obligation

When the full point reference is available at `production-mutation-runtime.cjs:1415-1418`, persist a versioned `outcome.failedPointRetirementCandidate` in the same transition as `restorePoint`. It contains the exact relative archive/sidecar names and immutable point reference fields needed for exact verification. This is only a candidate locator, **not deletion permission**. Continue to use `restoreCreation` for interruption before staging. Successful operations keep their point; the candidate marker never makes completed history eligible.

On authenticated terminal history discovery, include marked in-scope failed records in a separate `pendingRestorePointRetirements` result before the current `already-published` skip. Distinguish `authorized-retry` from `deferred-proof`: only the former may reach deletion. Reuse the registry's existing metadata traversal; do not scan profiles, all servers, backup names, or candidate/rollback roots. A crash after finish but before a receipt leaves a discoverable obligation, not newly inferred deletion permission. Existing records without exact locator evidence remain preserved; migration is out of scope and cannot be inferred from observed pairs.

### Receipt, distinct from terminal record

One receipt per failed job needs only:

- schema/policy version; failed job/operation kind; exact target binding; immutable published terminal-record digest and target-envelope binding;
- canonical parent/target/backup-directory identities, exact relative archive and sidecar names; point ID, archive digest/size, creator and the retained failed-operation pin owner;
- unchanged sidecar digest and exact archive/sidecar identities; no intended unpinned metadata state or replacement guard-pin owner;
- authenticated final settlement/policy disposition and caller provenance; monotonic progress (`authorized`, `deleting`, `archive-deleted`, `deleted`), exact deletion authorization and per-file durability result; retry/defer diagnostics without overwriting progress;
- no arbitrary root, glob, recursive-delete request, or UI-supplied path.

The receipt authenticates through the same guarded registry/binding/durability model as existing recovery evidence; calling it authenticated does not mean it is cryptographically signed against a malicious local administrator.

Create `authorized` only after the ownership policy passes. Under the active ambient coordinator lease, take the backup lock and revalidate before every effect. Receipt writes use registry durability/CAS. Factor an **exact-only** path in `RestorePointService` that skips `#recoverPendingRestorePointsLocked`; reuse strict reference matching, digest verification, and single-link/reparse/identity checks without rewriting the sidecar. The production facade exposes the narrow operation, not arbitrary filesystem authority to the renderer. A valid receipt may wait while the source is running; do not mutate the pair until the target is safely available.

### Exact-pair deletion with pins retained

1. Durably authorize the exact archive and unchanged sidecar only after final settlement and reference proof. Keep the failed job's existing physical pin. There is no unpin call, collector-pin transfer, or sidecar CAS. The receipt makes the failed history reference archival while the pin continues guarding the physical pair.
2. On a safely available target, recheck exact identities, current lineage, owners/references, and authority under the same coordinator lease and backup lock. Persist `deleting` before the first destructive effect. Delete the authorized archive by handle, flush its pinned parent, then record `archive-deleted`.
3. Recheck surviving sidecar identity/digest and protective references; delete that exact sidecar by handle, flush, and record `deleted`. Physical pin disappearance occurs with sidecar deletion. Never remove the backups directory or any sibling. A surviving pinned sidecar after archive removal is an explicit partial result, not broad-cleanup permission.
4. After an effect/flush/progress-write interruption, absence is reconcilable only against pre-existing exact deletion authorization and current directory lineage. Flush the authenticated parent as required before declaring durable completion. Preserve substituted, hardlinked, redirected, ambiguous, independently owned, or unexpected survivors. Missing files without prior authorization do not prove success; similar JSON/digest alone does not authorize a replacement identity.

The in-process backup lock serializes cooperating calls; the retained physical pin prevents ordinary retention from taking over between attempts. The native operation supplies the leaf-substitution boundary. Never expose an unpinned surviving pair and then permit the weaker filename-based cleaner to finish it.

### Required native exact-file boundary

No inspected existing API meets this deletion contract. Add one internal asynchronous file-only capability (illustratively `deleteFileExactAsync(path, { expectedFileIdentity, expectedParentIdentity })`) through `electron/utils/durable-fs.cjs`, `electron/utils/windows-durable-fs-client.cjs`, and the existing `native/ArchiveWorker` durability protocol. The expectation comes from the durable receipt, never from adopting a newly observed occupant. No recursive/batch/root deletion or renderer-supplied path is accepted.

Reuse `DurableRootPins`, `DurableAncestorPins`, and `DurablePinnedObject` to pin the exact parent/allowed lineage and open the leaf with DELETE/read access while denying write/delete sharing. Verify file type, `NumberOfLinks == 1`, expected identity/size/timestamps/hash, path binding, and existing reparse policy on the held handle. The current native snapshot does not itself enforce regular-file type or link count; the deletion path must. Do not silently replace the project's supported nonredirecting-reparse policy with a different threat model.

Use a handle-bound disposition, not a subsequent pathname unlink. Validate its supported Windows behavior with synthetic native tests; no behavior has been certified in this review. Completion requires confirmed deletion/close and pinned-parent flush, not merely delete-pending. Extend strict request/capability/failure-stage validation and distinguish pre-effect retries from uncertain post-effect completion. A lost response never permits deleting a replacement. Keep confirmed worker-close behavior before releasing serialization; ordinary retention failure is nonblocking, but an unproven live deleter is a writer-safety problem, not permission to overlap mutations or promise bounded latency.

This narrowly scoped native support is an accepted implementation prerequisite, subject to plan review and synthetic verification. If unsupported or unapproved, retain the pair and warn; a JS identity/hash precheck followed by pathname deletion is not an acceptable fallback. This does not claim resistance to a malicious local administrator or forged trusted authority.

Durability/partial deletion belongs here, not in the generic ordinary-backup cleaner. Do not call its public lock-taking `deleteBackup` from inside the same backup lock (nested acquisition would also deadlock).

## Concrete lifecycle and retry callers

### Original attempt

Add `mutationRuntime.retireFailedRestorePoint({jobId, plan, resolved, lease})` after successful `abandonCandidate` and required `restorePriorState`, before `#releaseClaim`. The lifecycle caller must explicitly establish that this invocation crossed its final policy-success boundary; arbitrary calls cannot infer that fact from arguments or history. Reauthenticate history even if recovery returned `resolved: true`. Use the original ambient lease, not a nested mutation. Persist authorization before attempting deletion, and defer pair mutation if the restored original is running. Do not route retention failure into rollback or label a proved original corrupt. Return distinct `reclaimed`, `deferred`, or `not-eligible` retention status; a pending receipt is not success. Surface a durable obligation/visible warning independently of the failed/cancelled operation's source-settlement outcome.

The first hook is confined to the `!commitStarted` precommit failure branch and strict `swapStep: none` predicate. `prepared` failures and all wider rollback branches are excluded in version one, even when disk evidence might support a future extension. Divergent-policy operations remain excluded even if a particular invocation happens to return successfully.

### Restart and recovery completion

Keep `retryTerminalPublication` entirely registry-metadata-only. After the existing startup recovery boundary, operation reconciliation, and successful main-window/navigation readiness, start one owned bounded housekeeping pass over **only** registry-derived obligations. Do not start or await archive proof/deletion in composition initialization, window creation, renderer readiness, or startup-window closure. Unreceipted candidates produce `deferred-proof` warnings, never deletion. For authorized receipts with an unblocked, available exact target, use ordinary `coordinator.runMutation` to retry. Do not fake a recovery block. Busy/running targets or unresolved recovery defer; cleanup must not stop/relaunch a server.

Root's plan-review refinement gives authorized retries their own budget: startup 16 retries plus 4 notice-only items (20 total); exact-target recovery completion 4 retries plus 2 notice-only items (6 total). Reclaimed/deferred-proof notices cannot consume retry slots. Order authorized receipts by never-attempted first, then oldest persisted attempt timestamp, with deterministic creation-time/job-ID ties; stamp attempts and deferred diagnostics through receipt CAS where possible. Successfully recorded busy attempts rotate across restart. Persistent I/O failure may prevent fairness metadata persistence, so no hard cleanup guarantee follows. This is a finite selection rule over existing per-job receipts, not a new queue/scheduler. Once closing starts, reject new passes, cancel unstarted dispatch, and drain active native ownership before shared-resource disposal; never exit orderly while a deleter's close is unproven. The precise contracts and regressions are in the executable plan.

After actual recovery completes and releases its lease/block, composition's `onResolved` can trigger the same exact-target retry of authorized receipts. An actual source-recovery caller may mint a new receipt under its existing lease only if its final policy boundary is separately proved for the same narrow disposition; no blanket hook in shared `#finish` and no minting from metadata-only retry. Such caller coverage must be explicit in the eventual implementation plan. Startup source recovery remains explicit, as today.

**Unreceipted crash gaps are conservatively deferred in version one.** Fully published `pre-swap-restored` proves recovery reached its source-policy boundary; for Repair, the outer call normally repeats the same cached operation/setting. Neither is a durable outer-success receipt or general current effective-policy proof. `getAutoRestartState(path)` applies config to module state; `readServerConfig` normalizes failures to defaults; current hold/timer/adoption caches are not durable policy proof. The collector must not use them to mint authorization after restart. Even a crash after outer success but before receipt creation remains deferred. Do not replay a stale whole-tree fingerprint, health-boot the original, change policy, or add a broad policy-query subsystem to avoid this limitation. Future unreceipted-gap coverage is a separate reviewed extension.

All archive/sidecar writes still need the new lease and backup lock. Bind a maintenance lease to its **current** maintenance operation ID and separately bind the retired job through the receipt; do not pass an expired failed-job lease or pretend the maintenance job is the old recovery operation. Backup-only mutation revision behavior must be explicit: a standalone maintenance mutation should use existing coordinator semantics consistently and invalidate/replan stale reviewed work if it advances content revision. Never mutate backups after capturing a new attempt's source fingerprint and then proceed with that stale proof.

### Best-effort behavior and availability

When final settlement is proven, authorization is durable, the target is available, and deletion/flush/receipt I/O succeeds, reclaim that attempt's exact pair without intentional delay. Ordinary backup settings or the creation of another ordinary backup do not control this path. If any condition fails, keep remaining material and expose why reclamation is pending; do not rewrite a safe original as corrupt or its prior recovery result as unsuccessful merely because retention failed.

There is **no retention-only admission gate**. Version one has no pre-planning/opportunistic admission-path retry: only the live final-policy hook, one owned post-ready startup pass, and the exact-target recovery-completion opportunity are in scope. Retained obligations do not refuse a new Repair. If separately authorized maintenance changes backup content or revision, replan/revalidate; never mutate a prior pair after capturing a new attempt's source fingerprint and then use the stale proof. Active/unproven writers, unresolved recovery, and insufficient capacity retain their independent safety meanings.

Each retry invocation considers a bounded set of known obligations and records deferred results; do not poll indefinitely or add a daemon. Persistent I/O failures, other owners, busy targets, or unreceipted crash gaps may accumulate full pairs even with healthy ordinary retention. No archive-count, byte, or cleanup-latency bound is claimed. Warnings must distinguish retained complete pairs, partial deletion, and missing final-policy proof without promising that every case will automatically drain.

## Implementation split

This is the accepted dependency/acceptance split, not the executable plan. Root reviews the separate plan before dispatch.

1. **Exact native file deletion boundary.** Extend only the existing durability stack/protocol; prove synthetic identity/link/path/share/disposition/close/flush behavior and uncertain completion. Deliver a strict async primitive that never falls back to pathname unlink. Later reclamation must not ship without this accepted boundary.
2. **Durable candidate and receipt authority.** Add candidate locator publication, exact terminal-evidence reads, and versioned per-job receipt validation/CAS/discovery. Prove immutable history, `authorized-retry` versus `deferred-proof`, independent-owner protection, and crash-safe progress. No server-file deletion yet.
3. **Exact pair orchestration.** Factor the no-discovery restorepoint verification path and add the helper using splits 1–2. Keep physical pins unchanged; archive-first then sidecar deletion, reference rechecks, current lineage, and missing/substituted-file reconciliation. Prove partial deletion and generic-cleaner refusal with synthetic fixtures.
4. **Production callers, warnings, and policy regression.** Wire the precommit final-policy hook and bounded post-ready startup/recovery-completion retries; no pre-planning retry in version one. Explicitly document actual source-recovery authorization coverage (none in v1; retry authorized receipts only). Prove that cleanup failure does not block another otherwise-safe Repair, unreceipted gaps remain retained, no process/policy actions occur for cleanup, and successful eligible flows actually reclaim archive bytes. Amend the accepted lifecycle specification only with root approval.

Each split needs its focused tests and review before integration; detailed signatures, test commands, runtime prerequisites, and execution method belong in the [executable plan](../plans/2026-10-11-failed-restorepoint-retirement.md), not this specification.

## Regression matrix (required evidence; not run)

Future tests must use synthetic temporary roots. Service fixtures use injected runtime/coordinator/archive services; native primitive tests use only generated files and the isolated durability worker against those roots. Production-instance directories, real recovery records/archives, profile discovery, and server-process control are forbidden test targets. Add a fixture-root assertion and verify no writes outside it. No test, build, or native execution is authorized or performed during this design amendment.

| Scenario | Required result |
| --- | --- |
| Repeated eligible precommit failures/cancellations; ordinary retention disabled; safely available target and healthy I/O | Actual production hook authorizes and deletes each exact pair; compact failed history remains. Assert archive bytes/count and physical pin preservation until deletion, not unpin calls. |
| Crash at each terminal record/envelope write/move | No pair mutation before full publication authentication; metadata-only retry never touches server files or mints authorization. Without a prior authorized receipt, preserve material as deferred proof. |
| Crash after final policy return but before receipt; Repair cached outer replay; divergent-policy uninstall | No receipt means conservative deferral even if outer success probably happened; divergent-policy operation remains excluded. Do not derive authority from current/defaulted config or a missing hold. |
| Crash after authorized receipt and before/after disposition, close, parent flush, or receipt progress | Existing obligation is discoverable/idempotent; reconcile absence only against prior exact authorization. Preserve a substituted survivor and a pinned partial sidecar; no ordinary cleaner takeover. |
| Actual startup composition and recovery-facade `onResolved` | Callers really invoke the helper; source startup recovery remains explicit; closed history uses ordinary maintenance authority, not fabricated recovery blocks. |
| Wrong/expired/nonambient lease, wrong job/target/path, busy/running target | Fail closed/defer without nested lease or process/policy changes; maintenance lease operation and retired job are separately authenticated. |
| Source/final-policy settlement fails, runtime writer unproven, quarantine, candidate remains, failed sibling, `prepared`/commit-started, between-renames, post-publication rollback | Keep pin and files; no false archival classification. Original server status must not be rewritten by a retention-only error. |
| Completed upgrade, successful restore, completed-history reference older than page 1, reviewed/claimed downgrade, another metadata pin/active recovery owner | Preserve pair and owners; full negative-reference proof required. Failed operation's own safety point must not be confused with its selected older point. |
| Directory/ancestor replacement, alias/junction, file-to-directory swap, hardlink, altered digest, corrupt/duplicate sidecar, receipt CAS conflict | Preserve material; native held-handle type/link/hash checks are exercised; no pending-point recovery or broad directory cleanup occurs. |
| Concurrent independent pin/reference, ordinary delete/rename, new lifecycle attempt, leaf substitution before/after native open | Consistent non-reentrant lock order; retain physical pin; refuse newly protected material; only the authenticated held leaf may be deleted. No path-only fallback. |
| Persistent deletion/flush/receipt error with repeated bounded retries | Durable retained/partial status and accurate warning; another otherwise-safe Repair remains admissible under normal safety/capacity checks. No hard storage-growth promise or source-corruption label. |
| Sharing conflict, worker timeout/lost response, unconfirmed worker close | No weaker deletion retry or overlapping mutation while a live deleter is unproven; report uncertainty separately from ordinary retained-backup failure. No bounded-latency claim. |
| Maintenance changes revision or backup content before an already-reviewed plan executes | Revalidation/replanning occurs; no stale full-source proof is reused. |
| Legacy failures without exact locator, empty/no-point failures, interrupted creation, completed marker records | Legacy preserved; no-point is a no-op; existing creation-intent cleanup remains separate; completed marker is never eligible. |
